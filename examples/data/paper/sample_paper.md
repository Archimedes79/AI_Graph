# Adaptive Watering Schedules Reduce Water Use in Urban Allotment Gardens

*A. Example, B. Placeholder — preprint, fictional sample for the paper review panel example*

## Abstract

Urban allotment gardens use large amounts of drinking water in summer. We tested an adaptive watering schedule that waters only when a soil-moisture sensor reads below a threshold. Across 12 plots over one season, the adaptive schedule reduced water use by 41% compared with fixed daily watering, with no loss in vegetable yield. These results prove that adaptive schedules should be mandatory for all urban gardens.

## 1. Introduction

Water use in community gardens has grown with the number of plots [1]. Fixed schedules water whether or not the soil needs it. Sensor-based irrigation is common in commercial agriculture [2], but has rarely been tested in small, shared plots where gardeners water by hand. Earlier work found savings between 20% and 30% in greenhouse settings [3].

## 2. Methods

We selected 12 plots in one allotment site. Six plots were assigned to the adaptive schedule and six to fixed daily watering; plots were assigned by the site manager according to which gardeners volunteered. Each adaptive plot had one capacitive soil-moisture sensor at 10 cm depth. When the reading fell below 25% volumetric water content, the gardener received a message and watered until the reading exceeded 35%. Water use was read from a meter at each plot tap every week from June to August. Yield was the total fresh weight of tomatoes, beans and courgettes harvested per plot.

## 3. Results

Mean weekly water use was 118 litres on fixed plots and 70 litres on adaptive plots (Table 1), a reduction of 41%. Yield did not differ between groups (Figure 1). One adaptive plot was excluded because its sensor failed in July.

## 4. Discussion

The adaptive schedule saved almost half of the water with no loss in yield, well above the savings reported for greenhouses [3]. Because the savings were large, the approach will work in any climate and for any crop. Gardeners reported that the messages were useful, which shows that the schedule is easy to adopt. A limitation is the single season.

## 5. Conclusion

Adaptive watering schedules cut water use by 41% without affecting yield and should be required in all urban gardens.
